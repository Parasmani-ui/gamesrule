## SIMULATION 1: FRUIT BEER GAME

### A Supply Chain Management Simulation

**🎯 PURPOSE & AIM**

- Demonstrate the **bullwhip effect** - a fundamental supply chain phenomenon
- Show how small demand fluctuations amplify upstream in multi-tier supply chains
- Teach the consequences of decentralized decision-making with limited information
- Illustrate the importance of supply chain coordination and information sharing

**📚 THEORETICAL FOUNDATION**

- **Core Concept:** Bullwhip Effect (Forrester Effect)
- **Theory Base:** Systems Dynamics, Supply Chain Management
- **Key Principle:** Demand amplification increases exponentially with each tier upstream
- **Formula:** Bullwhip Ratio = σ(Orders) / σ(Demand)

**🎓 LEARNING OBJECTIVES**

1. **Primary Learning:**
   - Understand how demand variability amplifies in supply chains
   - Recognize the role of lead times, batch ordering, and forecasting errors
   - Experience the "tragedy of the commons" in supply chain management

2. **Secondary Learning:**
   - Inventory management principles (safety stock, reorder points)
   - Cost trade-offs (holding costs vs. stockout costs)
   - Demand forecasting techniques
   - System thinking and feedback loops

**🎮 STRATEGIC CONCEPTS**

- **Individual Strategy:** Minimize personal costs (holding + stockout)
- **System Strategy:** Optimize entire supply chain performance
- **Conflict:** Individual optimization often leads to system sub-optimization
- **Solution:** Information sharing, collaboration, vendor-managed inventory (VMI)

**👥 TARGET AUDIENCE**

- **Primary:** MBA students, Supply Chain Management courses
- **Secondary:** Logistics professionals, Operations managers
- **Tertiary:** Undergraduate business students
- **Difficulty Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ Intermediate to Advanced

**💼 HELPFUL FOR**

- Supply chain coordinators designing information systems
- Inventory managers seeking to understand demand amplification
- Consultants advising on supply chain optimization
- Educators teaching systems thinking
- Anyone involved in multi-tier distribution networks

**🏆 KEY LEARNINGS**

1. **Operational:**
   - Order only what you need, not what you fear you'll need
   - Small batches reduce variability
   - Lead time reduction is critical

2. **Strategic:**
   - Information sharing eliminates bullwhip effect
   - Centralized control outperforms decentralized decision-making
   - Trust and collaboration are essential

3. **Behavioral:**
   - Fear of stockouts leads to overordering
   - Delayed feedback causes overcorrection
   - Individual rationality doesn't guarantee system optimality

**🌍 REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS**

- **Retail:** Walmart's vendor-managed inventory system
- **Manufacturing:** Toyota's just-in-time (JIT) production
- **E-commerce:** Amazon's demand forecasting algorithms
- **FMCG:** Procter & Gamble's collaborative planning

## SIMULATION 2: TOC SIMULATION

### Theory of Constraints in Operations Management

**🎯 PURPOSE & AIM**

- Teach Eliyahu Goldratt's **Theory of Constraints (TOC)** principles
- Demonstrate bottleneck identification and exploitation
- Show the impact of constraints on throughput, inventory, and operating expenses
- Illustrate the **Five Focusing Steps** methodology

**📚 THEORETICAL FOUNDATION**

- **Core Concept:** Theory of Constraints (TOC)
- **Theory Base:** Operations Management, Production Planning
- **Key Principle:** System performance is limited by its weakest link (constraint)
- **Philosophy:** Focus improvement efforts on the constraint for maximum impact

**🔑 THE FIVE FOCUSING STEPS**

1. **IDENTIFY** the system's constraint (bottleneck)
2. **EXPLOIT** the constraint (maximize its output)
3. **SUBORDINATE** all other processes to the constraint
4. **ELEVATE** the constraint (add capacity)
5. **REPEAT** - when constraint shifts, start over

**🎓 LEARNING OBJECTIVES**

1. **Primary Learning:**
   - Identify bottlenecks using utilization analysis
   - Apply Drum-Buffer-Rope (DBR) methodology
   - Understand the difference between throughput and efficiency

2. **Secondary Learning:**
   - Setup time reduction strategies
   - Batch sizing optimization
   - Work-in-progress (WIP) management
   - Multi-product production scheduling

**🎮 STRATEGIC CONCEPTS**

- **Drum:** Constraint sets production pace
- **Buffer:** Time buffer protects constraint from disruptions
- **Rope:** Material release synchronized to constraint's pace
- **Goal:** Maximize throughput while minimizing inventory and operating expenses

**💡 KEY INSIGHTS**

- **Hour Lost at Bottleneck = Hour Lost for Entire System**
- **Hour Saved at Non-Bottleneck = Mirage** (doesn't increase throughput)
- **Increasing Efficiency ≠ Increasing Throughput**
- **Local Optimization ≠ Global Optimization**

**👥 TARGET AUDIENCE**

- **Primary:** Operations management students, Industrial engineers
- **Secondary:** Production managers, Plant supervisors
- **Tertiary:** Lean Six Sigma practitioners
- **Difficulty Level:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Advanced

**💼 HELPFUL FOR**

- Manufacturing managers optimizing production lines
- Process improvement teams identifying constraints
- Supply chain planners balancing capacity
- Consultants implementing lean manufacturing
- Anyone managing capacity-constrained systems

**🏆 KEY LEARNINGS**

1. **Operational:**
   - Focus on throughput, not local efficiency
   - Idle time at non-bottlenecks is acceptable
   - Setup reduction at bottleneck is critical

2. **Strategic:**
   - Constraint determines system capacity
   - Elevating constraint requires capital investment
   - Continuous improvement must focus on constraints

3. **Financial:**
   - TOC metrics: Throughput, Inventory, Operating Expense
   - Traditional cost accounting can be misleading
   - Throughput accounting provides better decisions

**🌍 REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS**

- **Automotive:** Ford's production line optimization
- **Aerospace:** Boeing's assembly process management
- **Electronics:** Intel's fab capacity planning
- **Healthcare:** Hospital emergency department flow
- **Software:** DevOps pipeline optimization

## SIMULATION 3: EV GAMBIT

### A Strategy Simulation Using Porter's Five Forces

**🎯 PURPOSE & AIM**

- Apply **Michael Porter's Five Forces Framework** to competitive strategy
- Understand industry structure analysis and competitive dynamics
- Teach strategic decision-making in dynamic markets
- Demonstrate how external forces shape business strategy

**📚 THEORETICAL FOUNDATION**

- **Core Concept:** Porter's Five Forces Model (1979)
- **Theory Base:** Strategic Management, Competitive Analysis
- **Key Principle:** Industry profitability determined by five competitive forces
- **Context:** Indian Electric Vehicle (EV) industry

**🔍 THE FIVE FORCES**

**1. COMPETITIVE RIVALRY** (High in EV industry)
- Number of competitors (Evans, Electrify Inc., Tesla)
- Market growth rate (rapidly expanding)
- Product differentiation (technology, range, features)
- Exit barriers (high capital investment)

**2. THREAT OF NEW ENTRANTS** (Medium-High)
- Capital requirements (manufacturing facilities)
- Economies of scale (battery production)
- Government regulations (licensing, standards)
- Brand loyalty (established players have advantage)

**3. BARGAINING POWER OF SUPPLIERS** (High)
- Lithium supply concentration (few global suppliers)
- Battery component availability (semiconductor chips)
- Switching costs (high for specialized parts)
- Forward integration potential (suppliers could become competitors)

**4. BARGAINING POWER OF BUYERS** (Medium)
- Price sensitivity (Indian market very price-conscious)
- Government subsidies (increase buyer power)
- Product standardization (increasing comparability)
- Information availability (online reviews, comparisons)

**5. THREAT OF SUBSTITUTES** (Medium-High)
- Traditional ICE vehicles (still dominant)
- Ride-sharing services (Uber, Ola, SmartRide)
- Public transportation (improving infrastructure)
- Two-wheelers (major segment in India)

**🎓 LEARNING OBJECTIVES**

1. **Primary Learning:**
   - Analyze industry structure using Five Forces
   - Develop strategies to mitigate competitive threats
   - Understand how forces interact dynamically

2. **Secondary Learning:**
   - Strategic vs. operational vs. corporate decisions
   - Risk assessment and scenario planning
   - Competitive positioning strategies
   - Long-term strategic thinking

**🎮 STRATEGIC CONCEPTS**

- **Blue Ocean Strategy:** Avoid competition by creating uncontested market space
- **Cost Leadership:** Achieve lowest cost position
- **Differentiation:** Create unique value proposition
- **Focus Strategy:** Target specific market segment
- **Strategic Alliances:** Partner to strengthen position

**👥 TARGET AUDIENCE**

- **Primary:** MBA students, Strategy courses
- **Secondary:** Business strategists, Entrepreneurs
- **Tertiary:** Industry analysts, Consultants
- **Difficulty Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ Intermediate

**💼 HELPFUL FOR**

- Entrepreneurs entering new industries
- Strategy consultants conducting industry analysis
- Corporate strategists planning market entry
- Investors evaluating industry attractiveness
- Policy makers understanding industry dynamics

**🏆 KEY LEARNINGS**

1. **Analytical:**
   - Industry structure determines profitability potential
   - All five forces must be considered together
   - Forces change over time; analysis must be dynamic

2. **Strategic:**
   - Position firm to defend against strong forces
   - Exploit weak forces for competitive advantage
   - Strategic moves can reshape industry structure

3. **Tactical:**
   - Different strategies work in different force configurations
   - First-mover advantage isn't always beneficial
   - Government policy significantly impacts forces

**🌍 REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS**

- **EV Industry:** Tesla's market entry strategy
- **Smartphones:** Apple's differentiation vs. Xiaomi's cost leadership
- **Retail:** Amazon's disruption of traditional retail
- **Airlines:** Southwest's focus strategy
- **Pharmaceuticals:** Patent protection from new entrants

## SIMULATION 4: ORDER OPS

### The Online Food Delivery Simulation

**🎯 PURPOSE & AIM**

- Understand **platform business models** and two-sided markets
- Teach real-time logistics and fleet management
- Demonstrate trade-offs between cost, speed, and service quality
- Show how market structure affects competitive strategy

**📚 THEORETICAL FOUNDATION**

- **Core Concept:** Platform Economics, Network Effects
- **Theory Base:** Operations Management, Platform Strategy
- **Key Principle:** Platforms create value by facilitating interactions between producers and consumers
- **Context:** Online food delivery (OFD) ecosystem

**🏗️ PLATFORM ECOSYSTEM STRUCTURE**

```
SUPPLY SIDE          PLATFORM          DEMAND SIDE
Restaurants    ←→    Order Ops    ←→   Customers
                         ↕
                  Delivery Partners
```

**💡 KEY PLATFORM CONCEPTS**

**1. Network Effects**
- **Direct:** More customers attract more customers
- **Indirect:** More restaurants attract more customers (and vice versa)
- **Cross-side:** Growth on one side benefits other side

**2. Chicken-and-Egg Problem**
- Need restaurants to attract customers
- Need customers to attract restaurants
- Solution: Subsidize one side initially

**3. Multi-homing vs. Single-homing**
- Restaurants may list on multiple platforms (Swiggy, Zomato, Uber Eats)
- Customers may use multiple apps
- Creates competitive pressure

**🎓 LEARNING OBJECTIVES**

1. **Primary Learning:**
   - Manage multi-sided platform dynamics
   - Optimize delivery fleet in real-time
   - Balance commission rates with market share

2. **Secondary Learning:**
   - Route optimization algorithms
   - Dynamic pricing strategies
   - Service level management
   - Market structure impacts (monopoly/duopoly/oligopoly)

**🎮 STRATEGIC CONCEPTS**

- **Platform Strategy:** Build network effects and lock-in
- **Operational Excellence:** Minimize delivery time and cost
- **Pricing Strategy:** Balance commission revenue with competitiveness
- **Capacity Management:** Right-size delivery fleet

**📊 KEY PERFORMANCE INDICATORS (KPIs)**

- **Delivery Time:** Average time from order to delivery
- **Cost Per Delivery:** Total operational cost / number of deliveries
- **Customer Retention:** Repeat order rate
- **Restaurant Satisfaction:** Commission acceptance, order volume
- **Driver Utilization:** % of time drivers are actively delivering
- **Order Fulfillment Rate:** % of orders successfully delivered

**👥 TARGET AUDIENCE**

- **Primary:** Operations management students, Platform economics courses
- **Secondary:** Logistics managers, Platform entrepreneurs
- **Tertiary:** Supply chain analysts
- **Difficulty Level:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Advanced

**💼 HELPFUL FOR**

- Platform business founders (marketplace, sharing economy)
- Logistics coordinators in e-commerce
- Operations managers in gig economy companies
- Strategy consultants advising platform businesses
- Anyone managing multi-sided markets

**🏆 KEY LEARNINGS**

1. **Platform Economics:**
   - Winner-take-most dynamics in platform markets
   - Network effects create natural monopolies
   - Subsidizing one side can capture market

2. **Operations:**
   - Real-time optimization is critical
   - Trade-offs between speed, cost, and quality
   - Fleet size must match demand variability

3. **Strategy:**
   - Market structure dramatically affects profitability
   - Differentiation difficult in commodity platforms
   - Vertical integration (own fleet vs. gig workers) has trade-offs

**🌍 REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS**

- **Food Delivery:** Swiggy, Zomato, DoorDash, Uber Eats
- **Ride-hailing:** Uber, Lyft, Ola

## SIMULATION 5: TO PAY OR NOT TO PAY

### An HR Compensation & Hiring Simulation

**🎯 PURPOSE & AIM**

- Teach **Multi-Criteria Decision Making (MCDM)** in HR context
- Demonstrate expert system design and weight elicitation
- Show how subjective judgments can be systematized
- Illustrate the complexity of compensation decisions

**📚 THEORETICAL FOUNDATION**

- **Core Concept:** Multi-Attribute Decision Making (MADM)
- **Theory Base:** Decision Sciences, Human Resource Management
- **Key Principle:** Complex decisions can be decomposed into weighted criteria
- **Methods:** Weighted Sum Model (WSM), Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP)

**🎯 DECISION STRUCTURE**

**Stage 1: Expert Selection**
- Choose domain experts for compensation advice
- Experts have varying credibility scores
- Selection affects final recommendation weight

**Stage 2: Attribute Selection & Weighting**
- Identify relevant attributes (technical skills, leadership, experience, education)
- Assign importance weights (sum to 1.0)
- Weights reflect organizational priorities

**Stage 3: Candidate Ranking**
- Rank candidates based on weighted attributes
- Compare with optimal ranking
- Calculate compensation based on ranking

**💰 COMPENSATION CALCULATION**

```
Base Compensation: ₹5,00,000
Bonuses/Penalties:
+ Expert Selection Score (0 to ₹50,000)
+ Attribute Weight Score (0 to ₹1,00,000)
+ Ranking Match Score (0 to ₹3,00,000)
- Penalties for poor decisions
Final Package: ₹5,00,000 to ₹9,50,000
```

**🎓 LEARNING OBJECTIVES**

1. **Primary Learning:**
   - Apply Multi-Criteria Decision Making (MCDM) methods
   - Understand expert systems and credibility weighting
   - Learn attribute-based evaluation

2. **Secondary Learning:**
   - Compensation structure design
   - Ranking and scoring algorithms
   - Decision decomposition strategies
   - Bias recognition in hiring

**🎮 STRATEGIC CONCEPTS**

- **Expert Selection:** Choose high-credibility experts for better recommendations
- **Attribute Weighting:** Align weights with organizational strategy
- **Ranking Strategy:** Match candidate strengths to weighted criteria
- **Holistic Evaluation:** Consider multiple factors simultaneously

**👥 TARGET AUDIENCE**

- **Primary:** HR Management students, MBA programs
- **Secondary:** HR professionals, Recruiters, Hiring managers
- **Tertiary:** Compensation & Benefits specialists
- **Difficulty Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ Intermediate

**💼 HELPFUL FOR**

- HR managers designing compensation frameworks
- Recruiters making data-driven hiring decisions
- Consultants advising on talent acquisition
- Executives approving compensation packages
- Anyone involved in structured decision-making

**🏆 KEY LEARNINGS**

1. **Decision Science:**
   - Complex decisions benefit from structured approaches
   - Expert opinions should be weighted by credibility
   - Explicit criteria reduce bias

2. **HR Strategy:**
   - Compensation must align with organizational goals
   - Different roles require different attribute priorities
   - Transparency in criteria improves fairness

3. **Behavioral:**
   - Intuitive decisions often miss important factors
   - Systematic approaches outperform gut feelings
   - Documentation creates defensible decisions

**🌍 REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS**

## SIMULATION 6: SUSTAINABLE SELECT

### A Multi-Attribute Decision Making Simulation

**🎯 PURPOSE & AIM**

- Teach advanced **MADM algorithms** (WSM, WPM, TOPSIS, MOORA)
- Demonstrate trade-offs between conflicting criteria
- Show how different methods can yield different rankings
- Apply decision theory to sustainability challenges

**📚 THEORETICAL FOUNDATION**

- **Core Concept:** Multi-Attribute Decision Making (MADM)
- **Theory Base:** Operations Research, Decision Analysis
- **Key Principle:** Optimal choice from finite alternatives based on multiple criteria
- **Context:** Sustainable product selection (e.g., vehicle choice)

**🔢 THE FOUR MADM METHODS**

**1. Weighted Sum Model (WSM)**
```
Score(i) = Σ [w(j) × r(i,j)]
Where:
- w(j) = weight of criterion j
- r(i,j) = normalized performance of alternative i on criterion j
```
- **Simple and intuitive**
- **Best for:** Similar units across criteria
- **Limitation:** Assumes additive independence

**2. Weighted Product Model (WPM)**
```
Score(i) = Π [r(i,j)^w(j)]
Where:
- Product instead of sum
- Dimensionless comparison
```
- **No unit dependency**
- **Best for:** Criteria with different units
- **Limitation:** More complex to interpret

**3. TOPSIS (Technique for Order Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution)**
```
Steps:
1. Find ideal best (maximum benefit, minimum cost)
2. Find ideal worst
3. Calculate distance from both
4. Rank by relative closeness to ideal best
```
- **Considers both positive and negative ideals**
- **Best for:** Balanced decision-making
- **Limitation:** Computationally intensive

**4. MOORA (Multi-Objective Optimization on Basis of Ratio Analysis)**
```
Steps:
1. Normalize decision matrix
2. Calculate ratio system
3. Sum benefits - sum costs
4. Rank alternatives
```
- **Robust and simple**
- **Best for:** Multiple objectives with varying importance
- **Limitation:** Sensitive to outliers

**🎓 LEARNING OBJECTIVES**

1. **Primary Learning:**
   - Apply four different MADM algorithms
   - Understand when to use each method
   - Interpret and compare rankings from different methods

2. **Secondary Learning:**
   - Normalization techniques
   - Criteria identification and weighting
   - Sensitivity analysis
   - Trade-off analysis between objectives

**🌱 SUSTAINABILITY CONTEXT**

**Typical Decision Scenario:**
- **Alternatives:** Electric Car, Hybrid Car, Gasoline Car
- **Benefit Criteria:** Fuel efficiency, Range, Technology
- **Cost Criteria:** Purchase price, CO2 emissions, Maintenance cost
- **Weights:** Assigned based on buyer priorities

**🎮 STRATEGIC CONCEPTS**

- **Benefit Maximization:** Maximize desirable attributes
- **Cost Minimization:** Minimize undesirable attributes
- **Weight Sensitivity:** Small weight changes can alter rankings
- **Method Comparison:** Different methods reveal different insights

**👥 TARGET AUDIENCE**

- **Primary:** Operations Research students, Business Analytics courses
- **Secondary:** Decision analysts, Product managers
- **Tertiary:** Sustainability consultants, Policy makers
- **Difficulty Level:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Advanced

**💼 HELPFUL FOR**

- Product managers evaluating alternatives
- Sustainability officers making green choices
- Procurement specialists with multiple criteria
- Consultants conducting feasibility studies
- Anyone making complex multi-criteria decisions

**🏆 KEY LEARNINGS**

1. **Methodological:**
   - No single "best" method for all situations
   - Different methods emphasize different aspects
   - Sensitivity analysis is crucial

2. **Practical:**
   - Explicit criteria prevent biased decisions
   - Normalization ensures fair comparison
   - Weights must reflect true priorities

3. **Strategic:**
   - Trade-offs are inherent in complex decisions
   - Quantification forces clarity of thought
   - Documentation enables justification

**🌍 REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS**

- **Procurement:** Supplier selection (cost, quality, delivery, sustainability)
- **Real Estate:** Property selection (location, price, size, amenities)
- **Technology:** Software selection (features, cost, support, compatibility)

## SIMULATION 7: ONION DILEMMA

### A Game Theory Supply Chain Simulation

**🎯 PURPOSE & AIM**

- Teach **Game Theory** fundamentals in supply chain context
- Demonstrate the Prisoner's Dilemma in business settings
- Show how individual rationality leads to collective irrationality
- Illustrate coordination problems and trust issues

**📚 THEORETICAL FOUNDATION**

- **Core Concept:** Game Theory, Nash Equilibrium
- **Theory Base:** Economics, Strategic Interaction
- **Key Principle:** Optimal strategy depends on others' strategies
- **Context:** Onion farmers' pricing and production decisions

**🎲 GAME THEORY FUNDAMENTALS**

**Key Concepts:**
- **Players:** Farmers making production/pricing decisions
- **Strategies:** Plant more/less, price high/low
- **Payoffs:** Profit based on supply-demand equilibrium
- **Nash Equilibrium:** State where no player benefits from unilateral change

**📊 THE ONION DILEMMA STRUCTURE**

**Scenario:**
- Multiple onion farmers must decide production levels
- Market price depends on total supply
- High supply → Low price → Low profit (even with high volume)
- Low supply → High price → High profit (but requires coordination)

**Payoff Matrix (2-Player Example):**
```
                Farmer B: Low Production    Farmer B: High Production
Farmer A: Low   (High, High)               (Very Low, High)
Farmer A: High  (High, Very Low)           (Medium, Medium)
```

**The Dilemma:**
- **Cooperative Outcome:** Both produce low → Both profit high
- **Nash Equilibrium:** Both produce high → Both profit medium
- **Problem:** Fear of exploitation leads to suboptimal outcome

**🎓 LEARNING OBJECTIVES**

1. **Primary Learning:**
   - Understand Nash Equilibrium concept
   - Recognize Prisoner's Dilemma in business
   - Analyze strategic interactions

2. **Secondary Learning:**
   - Supply-demand dynamics
   - Market equilibrium mechanisms
   - Coordination mechanisms (contracts, cooperatives)
   - Trust-building strategies

**🎮 STRATEGIC CONCEPTS**

- **Dominant Strategy:** Best choice regardless of others' actions
- **Coordination Problem:** How to achieve cooperative outcome
- **Repeated Game:** Trust can emerge through repeated interactions
- **Communication:** Pre-game discussion can enable cooperation

**💡 KEY INSIGHTS**

**Why Cooperation Fails:**
1. **Lack of Trust:** Fear of being exploited
2. **Short-term Thinking:** Immediate gain vs. long-term benefit
3. **No Enforcement:** Cannot force others to cooperate
4. **Information Asymmetry:** Don't know others' intentions

**Solutions:**
1. **Contracts:** Binding agreements on production levels
2. **Cooperatives:** Centralized decision-making
3. **Reputation Systems:** Reward cooperators, punish defectors
4. **Government Intervention:** Production quotas, price supports

**👥 TARGET AUDIENCE**

- **Primary:** Economics students, Game Theory courses
- **Secondary:** Supply chain managers, Agricultural economists
- **Tertiary:** Policy makers, Business strategists
- **Difficulty Level:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Advanced

**💼 HELPFUL FOR**

- Economists analyzing market failures
- Supply chain coordinators managing commodity suppliers
- Policy makers designing agricultural support programs
- Business strategists in competitive markets
- Anyone facing coordination problems

**🏆 KEY LEARNINGS**

1. **Economic:**
   - Individual rationality ≠ Collective rationality
   - Market coordination requires mechanisms
   - Price volatility hurts all players

2. **Strategic:**
   - Cooperation requires trust and communication
   - Repeated interactions enable cooperation
   - Institutions can solve coordination problems

3. **Behavioral:**
   - Fear of loss drives defensive behavior
   - Short-term gain often trumps long-term benefit
   - Social norms can enforce cooperation

**🌍 REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS**

- **Agriculture:** OPEC oil production quotas, Coffee cartel coordination
- **Airlines:** Fare wars and capacity coordination
- **Technology:** Standards wars (VHS vs. Beta, Blu-ray vs. HD-DVD)
- **Environment:** Climate change negotiations (tragedy of the commons)
- **Labor:** Union collective bargaining

## SIMULATION 8: DUAL SOURCE DILEMMA

### A Procurement Strategy Game

**🎯 PURPOSE & AIM**

- Teach **procurement strategy** and supplier management
- Demonstrate trade-offs between single-sourcing and dual-sourcing
- Show risk mitigation through supplier diversification
- Illustrate cost-quality-reliability trade-offs

**📚 THEORETICAL FOUNDATION**

- **Core Concept:** Strategic Sourcing, Supply Risk Management
- **Theory Base:** Supply Chain Management, Procurement Strategy
- **Key Principle:** Balancing cost, risk, and quality in supplier selection
- **Context:** Manufacturing procurement decisions

**⚖️ SINGLE-SOURCING VS. DUAL-SOURCING**

**Single-Sourcing:**
- **Advantages:**
  - Lower unit costs (volume discounts)
  - Simplified relationships
  - Easier quality control
  - Lower administrative costs

- **Disadvantages:**
  - High supplier dependency
  - No leverage in negotiations
  - Vulnerable to disruptions
  - Limited innovation

**Dual-Sourcing:**
- **Advantages:**
  - Risk mitigation (supplier failure backup)
  - Competitive pressure on suppliers
  - Negotiation leverage
  - Access to diverse capabilities

- **Disadvantages:**
  - Higher unit costs (split volume)
  - More complex management
  - Inconsistent quality
  - Higher administrative burden

**🎓 LEARNING OBJECTIVES**

1. **Primary Learning:**
   - Evaluate single vs. dual sourcing strategies
   - Assess supplier risk profiles
   - Calculate total cost of ownership (TCO)

2. **Secondary Learning:**
   - Supplier qualification criteria
   - Contract negotiation strategies
   - Contingency planning
   - Performance monitoring

**🎮 STRATEGIC CONCEPTS**

- **Risk Diversification:** Don't put all eggs in one basket
- **Total Cost of Ownership:** Consider all costs, not just price
- **Supplier Relationship Management:** Strategic vs. transactional relationships
- **Capacity Allocation:** How to split orders between suppliers

**📊 DECISION FACTORS**

**Supplier Evaluation Criteria:**
1. **Cost:** Unit price, payment terms, discounts
2. **Quality:** Defect rates, consistency, certifications
3. **Reliability:** On-time delivery, capacity stability
4. **Risk:** Financial health, geopolitical factors, natural disasters
5. **Innovation:** R&D capabilities, technology leadership
6. **Responsiveness:** Flexibility, lead time, communication

**Sourcing Strategies:**
- **70/30 Split:** Primary supplier (70%) + Backup (30%)
- **50/50 Split:** Equal split for maximum leverage
- **Dynamic Allocation:** Shift based on performance
- **Sequential:** Start single, add second if risks materialize

**👥 TARGET AUDIENCE**

- **Primary:** Supply chain management students, Procurement courses
- **Secondary:** Procurement managers, Buyers
- **Tertiary:** Supply chain analysts, Category managers
- **Difficulty Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ Intermediate to Advanced

**💼 HELPFUL FOR**

## SIMULATION 9: DEFECT DETECTIVES

### A Quality Control Simulation

**🎯 PURPOSE & AIM**

- Teach **Statistical Quality Control (SQC)** principles
- Demonstrate **inspection strategies** and sampling plans
- Show cost trade-offs between inspection and defects
- Illustrate Type I and Type II errors in quality decisions

**📚 THEORETICAL FOUNDATION**

- **Core Concept:** Statistical Process Control (SPC), Six Sigma
- **Theory Base:** Quality Management, Statistical Methods
- **Key Principle:** Variation is inherent; control limits separate common from special causes
- **Context:** Manufacturing quality inspection

**📊 QUALITY CONTROL FUNDAMENTALS**

**Key Concepts:**
- **Defect:** Non-conformance to specification
- **Inspection:** Examination to detect defects
- **Sampling:** Inspecting subset to infer about population
- **Control Chart:** Statistical tool to monitor process variation

**Types of Errors:**
- **Type I Error (α):** False positive - Rejecting good batch (Producer's risk)
- **Type II Error (β):** False negative - Accepting bad batch (Consumer's risk)
- **Trade-off:** Reducing one error type increases the other

**Inspection Strategies:**
1. **100% Inspection:** Check every unit (expensive but thorough)
2. **Sampling Inspection:** Check random sample (cost-effective but risky)
3. **No Inspection:** Accept all (cheap but high risk)

**Control Charts:**
```
Upper Control Limit (UCL) = μ + 3σ
Center Line (CL) = μ
Lower Control Limit (LCL) = μ - 3σ
Where:
μ = Process mean
σ = Process standard deviation
```

**🎓 LEARNING OBJECTIVES**

1. **Primary Learning:**
   - Design sampling plans using AQL/LTPD
   - Construct and interpret control charts
   - Calculate inspection costs vs. defect costs

2. **Secondary Learning:**
   - Statistical significance testing
   - Process capability analysis (Cp, Cpk)
   - Root cause analysis
   - Continuous improvement methodologies

**🎮 STRATEGIC CONCEPTS**

- **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Inspection cost vs. cost of passing defects
- **Risk Management:** Balance Type I and Type II errors
- **Prevention vs. Detection:** Upstream quality control is cheaper
- **Statistical Confidence:** Higher confidence requires larger samples

**💰 COST STRUCTURE**

```
Total Quality Cost = Prevention + Appraisal + Internal Failure + External Failure

Prevention Cost: Training, process design, quality planning
Appraisal Cost: Inspection, testing, audit
Internal Failure: Scrap, rework, downtime (found before customer)
External Failure: Warranty, returns, reputation damage (found by customer)
```

**👥 TARGET AUDIENCE**

- **Primary:** Quality management students, Industrial engineering courses
- **Secondary:** Quality engineers, Manufacturing managers
- **Tertiary:** Six Sigma practitioners, Process improvement teams
- **Difficulty Level:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Advanced

**💼 HELPFUL FOR**

- Quality control managers designing inspection systems
- Manufacturing engineers optimizing quality processes
- Six Sigma Black Belts conducting DMAIC projects
- Operations managers balancing cost and quality
- Anyone responsible for product quality assurance
- **Laboratory technicians** conducting clinical QC (DMLT professionals!)

**🏆 KEY LEARNINGS**

1. **Statistical:**
   - All processes have variation (common cause vs. special cause)
   - Control limits are statistical, not specification limits
   - Larger samples provide more confidence but cost more

2. **Economic:**
   - 100% inspection isn't always optimal
   - Prevention is cheaper than detection
   - Cost of quality follows the "iceberg model"

3. **Strategic:**
   - Quality is built in, not inspected in
   - Data-driven decisions outperform intuition
   - Continuous improvement requires measurement

**🌍 REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS**

- **Automotive:** Toyota's Jidoka (automation with human touch)
- **Pharmaceuticals:** FDA-mandated sampling and testing protocols
- **Electronics:** Semiconductor wafer testing strategies

## SIMULATION 10: CUSTOMER IN A STORE

### A Stock-Flow Dynamics Simulation

**🎯 PURPOSE & AIM**

- Teach **stock-flow dynamics** and systems thinking
- Demonstrate the **correlation heuristic** - a common cognitive bias
- Show why experts struggle with seemingly simple problems
- Illustrate the difference between stocks and flows

**📚 THEORETICAL FOUNDATION**

- **Core Concept:** Stock-Flow Reasoning, Cognitive Biases
- **Theory Base:** Systems Dynamics, Behavioral Economics
- **Key Principle:** Humans naturally confuse flows (rates) with stocks (accumulations)
- **Context:** Customer traffic in retail store

**🧠 THE CORRELATION HEURISTIC**

**The Problem:**
People incorrectly assume stocks (inventory, customers in store) follow the same pattern as flows (orders, customer arrivals).

**Example:**
```
Question: "When is the number of customers in the store at maximum?"
Common (Wrong) Answer: "When customer arrivals peak"
Correct Answer: "When cumulative (arrivals - departures) peaks"
This typically occurs AFTER arrival rate peaks!
```

**Why This Happens:**
- **Pattern Matching:** Brain seeks correlations
- **Intuitive Physics:** Confuse velocity with position
- **Cognitive Load:** Integration (calculus) is mentally taxing
- **Education Gap:** More algebra taught than calculus

**📐 MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATION**

**Stock-Flow Relationship:**
```
Stock(t) = Stock(t-1) + Inflow(t) - Outflow(t)

Or in continuous form:
dStock/dt = Inflow(t) - Outflow(t)

Maximum Stock occurs when: Inflow = Outflow (crossover point)
NOT when Inflow is maximum!
```

**🎓 LEARNING OBJECTIVES**

1. **Primary Learning:**
   - Understand stock vs. flow distinction
   - Recognize and overcome correlation heuristic
   - Develop systems thinking capabilities

2. **Secondary Learning:**
   - Interpret time series graphs correctly
   - Apply integration concepts to business
   - Identify cognitive biases in decision-making

**🔬 THREE LEARNING INTERVENTIONS**

**Group 1: Learning-by-Doing**
- Solve multiple variations of the problem
- Build intuition through repetition
- Implicit learning approach

**Group 2: Task Complexity Decomposition**
- Break problem into smaller components
- Learn step-by-step reasoning
- Explicit instruction approach

**Group 3: Binary Feedback**
- Immediate right/wrong feedback
- Fast error correction
- Reinforcement learning approach

**Research Question:** Which intervention is most effective?

**🎮 STRATEGIC CONCEPTS**

- **Systems Thinking:** Understand dynamic systems behavior
- **Visual Interpretation:** Read graphs correctly
- **Cognitive Awareness:** Recognize your own biases
- **Mental Models:** Build accurate representations

**👥 TARGET AUDIENCE**

- **Primary:** Decision sciences students, Behavioral economics courses
- **Secondary:** Managers making data-driven decisions
- **Tertiary:** Anyone interpreting business metrics
- **Difficulty Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ Intermediate (concept is simple, but counterintuitive)

**💼 HELPFUL FOR**

- Analysts interpreting time series data
- Managers reading financial/operational reports
- Forecasters predicting future states
- Educators teaching systems thinking
- Anyone working with accumulation metrics

**🏆 KEY LEARNINGS**

1. **Cognitive:**
   - Human intuition fails on stock-flow problems
   - Systematic approaches outperform intuition
   - Even experts make these mistakes

2. **Practical:**
   - Always distinguish stocks from flows
   - Peak flow ≠ Peak stock
   - Integration matters in business decisions

3. **Educational:**
   - Different learning methods suit different people
   - Feedback accelerates learning
   - Deliberate practice overcomes biases

**🌍 REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS**

- **Inventory Management:** Stock on hand vs. order rates
- **Finance:** Cash balance vs. revenue/expenses
- **Population:** Total population vs. birth/death rates
- **Water Management:** Reservoir level vs. inflow/outflow
- **Debt:** Total debt vs. borrowing/repayment rates
- **Epidemiology:** Infected population vs. infection/recovery rates

**📊 BUSINESS EXAMPLES:**

- **Misinterpretation:** "Sales are increasing, so we're profitable!"
  - **Reality:** Cash balance (stock) may still be declining if expenses > sales

- **Misinterpretation:** "New hires peaked last month, so headcount is highest!"
  - **Reality:** Headcount peaks when hires = departures, not when hires peak

## SIMULATION 11: DEMAND FORECAST CHALLENGE

### A Time-Series Forecasting Simulation

**🎯 PURPOSE & AIM**

- Teach **time-series forecasting** methods
- Compare accuracy of different forecasting techniques
- Demonstrate importance of forecast error metrics
- Show how data patterns affect method selection

**📚 THEORETICAL FOUNDATION**

- **Core Concept:** Time-Series Forecasting
- **Theory Base:** Statistics, Operations Research
- **Key Principle:** Past patterns help predict future demand
- **Context:** Retail demand forecasting

**📈 FORECASTING METHODS**

**1. Naive Forecast**
```
F(t+1) = A(t)
Next period = Current period
```
- Simplest method
- Baseline for comparison
- Works if data is random

**2. Moving Average (MA)**
```
F(t+1) = [A(t) + A(t-1) + ... + A(t-n+1)] / n
Average of last n periods
```
- Smooths out random fluctuations
- Lags behind trends
- Good for stable demand

**3. Weighted Moving Average (WMA)**
```
F(t+1) = w₁×A(t) + w₂×A(t-1) + ... + wₙ×A(t-n+1)
Where Σwᵢ = 1
```
- More weight on recent data
- More responsive than simple MA
- Requires weight selection

**4. Exponential Smoothing (ES)**
```
F(t+1) = α×A(t) + (1-α)×F(t)
Where 0 < α < 1 (smoothing constant)
```
- Weighted average of all past data
- Recent data weighted more
- Only needs α parameter
- Memory-efficient

**5. Double Exponential Smoothing (Holt's Method)**
- Captures trend
- Two equations: level and trend
- Better for trending data

**6. Linear Regression**
```
Y = a + b×t
Where:
a = intercept
b = slope (trend)
t = time period
```
- Fits linear trend line
- Simple and interpretable
- Good for steady trends

**📊 FORECAST ACCURACY METRICS**

**1. Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD)**
```
MAD = Σ|A(t) - F(t)| / n
```
- Average absolute error
- Same units as data
- Easy to interpret

**2. Mean Squared Error (MSE)**
```
MSE = Σ[A(t) - F(t)]² / n
```
- Penalizes large errors more
- Squared units
- Statistical properties

**3. Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE)**
```
MAPE = (Σ|[A(t) - F(t)] / A(t)|) / n × 100%
```
- Percentage terms
- Scale-independent
- Industry standard

**4. Tracking Signal**
```
TS = Σ[A(t) - F(t)] / MAD
```
- Detects bias
- Should be between -4 and +4
- Signals when to change method

**🎓 LEARNING OBJECTIVES**

1. **Primary Learning:**
   - Apply 6 different forecasting methods
   - Calculate forecast error metrics
   - Select appropriate method for data patterns

2. **Secondary Learning:**
   - Identify time-series patterns (trend, seasonality, randomness)
   - Understand bias vs. variance trade-off
   - Tune forecasting parameters

**🎮 STRATEGIC CONCEPTS**

- **Method Selection:** Match method to data characteristics
- **Parameter Tuning:** Optimize α, n, weights
- **Error Monitoring:** Track performance continuously
- **Model Updates:** Refresh with new data

**📊 DATA PATTERNS**

**1. Stationary (Horizontal)**
- No trend or seasonality
- Random fluctuations around mean
- **Best Methods:** Moving Average, Exponential Smoothing

**2. Trending**
- Consistent upward or downward movement
- **Best Methods:** Double Exponential Smoothing, Regression

**3. Seasonal**
- Regular periodic patterns
- **Best Methods:** Seasonal decomposition, Seasonal indices

**4. Random (White Noise)**
- No pattern
- **Best Method:** Naive (all methods perform similarly)

**👥 TARGET AUDIENCE**

- **Primary:** Operations management students, Analytics courses
- **Secondary:** Demand planners, Supply chain analysts
- **Tertiary:** Data scientists, Business analysts
- **Difficulty Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ Intermediate

**💼 HELPFUL FOR**

- Demand planners in manufacturing/retail
- Supply chain analysts forecasting demand
- Inventory managers setting stock levels
- Financial analysts projecting sales
- Anyone responsible for planning based on historical data

**🏆 KEY LEARNINGS**

1. **Methodological:**
   - No single "best" forecasting method
   - Method selection depends on data pattern
   - Simple methods often outperform complex ones

2. **Practical:**
   - Forecast accuracy diminishes with horizon
   - Regular monitoring and updating is crucial
   - Combine quantitative with qualitative inputs

3. **Strategic:**
   - Better forecasts → Lower inventory costs
   - Forecast errors drive safety stock levels
   - Collaborative forecasting improves accuracy

**🌍 REAL-WORLD APPLICATIONS**

- **Retail:** Walmart's demand forecasting for 100M+ SKUs
- **Manufacturing:** Toyota's production planning
- **E-commerce:** Amazon's inventory positioning
- **Utilities:** Electricity demand forecasting
- **Finance:** Stock price prediction (with caveats!)
- **Healthcare:** Patient admission forecasting

**⚠️ IMPORTANT CAVEATS**

- **Forecasting Limitations:**
  - Cannot predict disruptions (COVID-19, strikes)
  - Historical patterns may not continue
  - External factors (promotions, weather) not captured
  
- **Best Practices:**
  - Use multiple methods and average
  - Include domain expertise
  - Update models regularly
  - Track and learn from errors
