The Game
This simulation is designed to immerse you in the Indian Electric Vehicle (EV) industry, a rapidly evolving sector influenced by changing consumer preferences, technological advancements, and government policies.Through this game, you will step into the shoes of a decision-maker navigating the competitive landscape. Using Porter’s Five Forces framework, you will analyze how the interplay between competitive rivalry, the threat of new entrants, the bargaining power of suppliers and buyers, and the threat of substitutes influences strategic choices.The goal is to help you develop a practical understanding of how industry forces shape firm behavior, strategy, and long-term profitability.

---

How to Play
This is a single-player game.You will take on the role of the focal firm operating in the Indian EV industry.The game is played over five rounds.In each round, a new event will be introduced that affects the business environment — these events could come from suppliers, buyers, competitors, new entrants, substitutes, or regulators.After each event, you must make strategic decisions that align with your understanding of Porter’s Five Forces.Your goal is to adapt, compete, and thrive by effectively responding to the dynamic forces shaping the industry.

Focal Firm
EVans is an EV two and four-wheeler manufacturing company. Their market share currently is 5%, but hold 35% share within the EV market and they expect rapid growth in the Indian market.  EVans’ main competitor is Electrify Inc. which currently holds 12% in the automobile segment and 35% in the EV segment. EVans sources their batteries from China from a supplier firm named LiOn along with a Russian firm, Rusloth; the split is 60-40. However, as India has discovered Lithium reserves, they have invested in a firm called India Mines that would sell them the batteries in next 2 years. Currently, EVans’ biggest buyer is Rexa, which is an EV-based cab service, and it holds 40% market share in cabs industry. They buy 50% of EVans’ vehicles. Next biggest is UShuttle, which is an EV shuttle service for office goers and caters to 25% of office commuters and boast to serve organizations such as Google and Microsoft.

Competitor
Electrify Inc. is an automobile company started three decades ago. They are known for adopting latest technologies in their vehicles. It’s a family business and the promoters are in the richest businesspersons in the world. Electrify Inc. solely relies on LiOn for their Lithium supply. They currently manufacture vehicles for businesses and passengers and do not sell anything to the government. Electrify’s price point is usually 10-15% higher than EVans’ vehicles.

Supplier
LiOn is a state-sponsored company that supplies Lithium as well as customized batteries to firms dealing in EVs. They have two clients in India and in talks with three other companies, which they hope to convert as the demand for EVs is on the rise.

New Entrant
Tesla is a renowned company that explores emerging technologies such as hyperloop and SpaceX and it also manufactures EVs in the US. Tesla is setting up a plant in India to manufacture EVs and start selling them after 5 years from now. The government is happy about this as it will attract more such businesses to enter India.

Buyer
EVans’ biggest buyer is Rexa, which is an EV-based cab service, and it holds 40% market share in cabs industry. They buy 50% of EVans’ vehicles. Next biggest is UShuttle, which is an EV shuttle service for office goers and caters to 25% of office commuters and boast to serve organizations such as Google and Microsoft.

Substitute
SmartRide is an Indian unicorn that is promoting ride sharing to curb vehicular pollution. They have tied up with major companies and government departments so that people don’t have to buy cars and two wheelers and also not rely on overburdened public transport. They have been enjoying more than 10% CAGR since they have started and cater to more than 10 million customers in a year.
